## TL;DR
> A beginner walkthrough for accessing ByteDance's Seedance 2.0 video model through the BytePlus Playground: signing up, verifying with a phone number, activating the model with payment details, and generating a first video. The second half covers prompting best practices — camera-motion transfer from a reference video, keeping to one or two character images for consistency, and stating durations explicitly.

## Key Takeaways
- Seedance 2.0 is accessed via the BytePlus Playground Arena (link in description), currently labeled "experience only" (pre-final version).
- Sign-up requires a BytePlus account (Google sign-in supported) plus a real, verifiable phone number.
- New accounts get 5 million free tokens, but usage is consumed faster at higher resolutions/output lengths.
- The model must be explicitly "activated" (confirm authorization + add a payment method) before it can generate.
- Seedance 2.0 is also reachable via a chat interface, but that route currently requires an invite code, and generations there can take 15-20 minutes due to high demand.
- A key prompting technique: motion/camera movement from a reference video can be applied to a completely different single image, transferring camera movement and scene-transition rhythm without describing the motion manually.
- Best consistency comes from using only one or two character images per video; using more (unlike Nano Banana Pro, which can juggle around 14 subjects) risks the model getting confused and identity/consistency degrading — using characters the model already has internal knowledge of (e.g. Thanos, Captain America) also helps.
- Stating exact durations in prompts (e.g. "walks for 3 seconds, stops, then turns for 2 seconds") improves control over timed actions.
- The presenter argues Seedance 2.0 largely removes prior video-model bottlenecks (only minor blurriness in text/numbers remains), undercutting the $600-$4,000 typical cost of professional motion graphics work.
